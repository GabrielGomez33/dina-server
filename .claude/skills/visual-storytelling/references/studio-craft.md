# Studio craft — the sourced deep-dive

The "how the studios actually do it" behind `SKILL.md`. Read this when you want the underlying *why*,
concrete technique, or studio examples to emulate. Part 1 (visual & animation craft of emotion) is
below; Part 2 (story structure + the emotional story spines) follows. All sources accessed 2026-09-22.

> **One-line diagnosis of flat attempts:** we were making *cards* (static, uniform, caption-dependent),
> not an *arc*. The fix is **change-over-time on five vectors at once** — cool→warm, dark→light,
> far→close, slumped→lifted, fast→slow-at-the-peak — plus a held "take" before the payoff and a living
> micro-motion in every clip so nothing reads as dead. A still frame can't be emotional; a *sequence
> that changes* can.

# PART 1 — The visual & animation craft of emotion

## 1. The 12 principles, only the ones that carry emotion

- **Appeal (#1 lever).** Why we care before anything happens. Little One has the "cute formula" (big
  eyes, round compact body, soft shapes = infant proportions → nurturing response). Protect it in
  *every* keyframe: silhouette reads in one glance, line-of-action curved (not vertical-rigid),
  asymmetry = life (perfect symmetry = a dead mascot logo).
- **Staging = clarity of the emotional idea.** One beat, one feeling. Biggest flatness cause is two
  ideas in a frame, neither staged. Say the beat's emotion in one word; if the muted keyframe doesn't
  say it, restage — don't lean on a caption.
- **Anticipation = the wind-up that makes a reaction land.** A smaller opposite motion first (shrink
  before rising, lean back before reaching). Encode as a 2-keyframe beat: A = anticipation pose, B =
  the action ("dips down and gathers in, then springs upward").
- **Timing & spacing = speed IS the feeling.** Slow, evenly-spaced = calm/sad/weight; fast, tight =
  excitement/fear/joy. Always ease ("slowly settles"), never constant-velocity (robotic/dead). Assign
  each beat a speed word.
- **Exaggeration = push past literal.** Real emotion under-reads on a simple creature. Author poses at
  ~120–150% of realistic — droop lower, lift higher than gravity wants.
- **Squash & stretch = aliveness + mood.** Squashed wider/lower = heavy/deflated/sad; stretched-up =
  alert/eager/joyful. Keep volume so it reads as living tissue.
- **Secondary action = supporting detail.** One small extra per beat (a blink, ear twitch, breath
  release) deepens the main feeling without adding a new idea.
- **Follow-through & overlap = nothing stops at once.** Soft parts (ears, tuft, cheeks) keep moving a
  beat after the body stops, then settle at slightly different times. Perfect for a soft creature.

## 2. Acting & micro-expression — "the eyes are everything"

~70–80% of emotion reads in the face, ~90% of that in the eyes. For a near-featureless creature the
eyes + a few soft parts + posture are the *entire* toolkit.

- **Eye size:** wide = fear/surprise/wonder/hope; small/narrowed = sad/sleepy/content. Change size
  *across a cut* to show a shift.
- **Eyeline:** down = sad/defeated/shy; up = hope/wonder/longing; off-frame = anticipation of arrival.
  **A change of eyeline is a thought** — down→up is a whole "sad→hopeful" beat by itself.
- **Blink for a reason.** Slow blink = calm/acceptance/sadness; fast double-blink = surprise. Blink on
  a beat-change to mark the moment of realization.
- **Catchlight:** a tiny highlight in the eye = life/hope; dim it for despair, restore it bigger/
  brighter at the turn.
- **Soft parts & posture:** ears/tuft drooped=sad, perked=hopeful, flattened=fear; rosy cheeks brighten
  with warmth returning. **Slump vs lift is the spine of wordless acting** (curled-in/sunk/small =
  lonely; opened/lifted/tall = hope). A held breath then release = the emotional exhale.
- **Rule:** stack **≥3 agreeing channels** (eyes + ears + posture + light). One channel is ambiguous;
  three are unmistakable — exactly what you need with no words.

## 3. The pause / held moment / "the take" — the biggest missing ingredient

**More cuts ≠ more emotion. Fewer, longer, well-timed holds = more emotion.** Cutting fast denies the
audience time to feel.

- **"The beat before" (the take):** insert a held moment of stillness *before* a reaction — see the
  thing → HOLD (wide still eyes, 0.8–1.5s) → *then* the slow turn into feeling. Instant reactions read
  as "not processing."
- **Let it breathe:** after the payoff, hold the resolved image an extra beat. A close image held "a
  second too long" tips neutral → tender; cut a second early and you kill it.
- A hold is still a **moving hold** (§7): near-frozen, just breathing + a blink. Give holds the
  **longest** durations (1.2–2.5s+).

## 4. Color & light as emotion (the Pixar color script)

Pixar maps a film's whole emotional arc in **color/light thumbnails** before animating — the color
progression tells the story with no characters. Build a tiny color script per 20s short: **decide the
palette journey first, beat by beat.**

- **Cool** (blue/blue-grey/teal, desaturated, low-key) = loneliness/isolation/sadness/distance → your
  *opening*. **Warm** (amber/gold/rose/peach) = safety/comfort/intimacy/hope/love → your *payoff*.
- **The color TURN = the payoff.** Cold→warm: start blue/desaturated/dark & small, let warmth *arrive*
  (a warm source appears, blooms, floods Little One). Classic staging: **a warm-lit character against a
  cool surround** — the one warm thing in a cold frame is instantly the emotional focal point.
- **Mini color script, sad→hopeful:** (1) desaturated cool charcoal-blues, low key, Little One barely
  visible in dark; (2) one small warm accent appears (an ember/amber dot); (3) warm source grows,
  saturation & contrast creep up, cheeks catch light; (4) warm gold floods, fully lit, highest warmth/
  saturation lands here. Value arc: dark/low-contrast → lit/high-contrast (literal darkness→light).

## 5. Composition & staging for feeling (vertical-specific)

- **Small in a big empty frame = loneliness/vulnerability.** Tiny figure, tall column of empty cool/
  dark space above (vertical 9:16 is *perfect*): emptiness "presses down" = isolation, and leaves room
  for warmth to descend later. Use for the opening.
- **Filling the frame (close-up) = intimacy.** Push in close on the eyes for the payoff so the viewer
  shares it. tiny-in-frame → close-up across the piece *is* the alone→connected arc.
- **Camera height:** high-angle-down = smaller/weaker/vulnerable (sad low point); eye-level/low =
  dignity/with-them (hopeful rise).
- **Eyeline up** at the light/thing = longing; give the thing its own frame space so the gaze lands.
- **Depth:** a dark foreground pocket deepens isolation; opening it as warmth arrives = release.
- Compose on **vertical thirds**; lonely creature *low*, hope/light *high* → then they meet.

## 6. Pacing, rhythm & music

Emotion is waves: tension/anticipation → release. **Uniform durations = slideshow; vary them.**

- Cut on beats; land the big pose/color-turn on the **musical swell**; sit a hold on a **rest/silence**.
- **Ritardando at the peak:** slow everything approaching the climax — the payoff beat is the slowest,
  longest. Speeding up = excitement; slowing = "feel this."
- A beat of near-silence before the payoff makes the swell hit harder.
- **Proven 7-beat shape (~20s), durations varied, ~7 cuts total (restraint is the point):**

  | Beat | Function | Feel | Duration |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | Establish — Little One small, cold, alone | loneliness | 3–4s |
  | 2 | Something noticed / a small change | curiosity, unease | 2–2.5s |
  | 3 | **The take — held beat before reaction** | held breath | 2–3s (near-still) |
  | 4 | The turn — warmth/light arrives | dawning hope | 2.5–3s |
  | 5 | Rise — reaching, lifting, opening | hope building | 2–2.5s |
  | 6 | **Payoff — warm flood, close-up, slowest** | joy/safety/love | 3–4s (ritardando) |
  | 7 | Resolve — hold on the warm image | afterglow | 2–3s |

## 7. Continuity of motion & "aliveness"

- **Nothing fully frozen** — a static frame reads as dead. Every beat (even holds) gets a **moving
  hold**: breathing (~1–2cm), tuft/ears drift, occasional blinks, ambient drift (dust motes, soft
  particles, slow light flicker). Prompt at least one per clip.
- **Match action across cuts:** eyeline, pose direction, and the color progression carry over (a hair
  warmer/brighter each beat) — this is what makes 7 clips feel like *one* experience (the likely cause
  of our disjointed feel).
- **Transformation reads as emotion:** something appearing/clearing/blooming/igniting = change toward
  life = inherently hopeful. Use a **verb of becoming** ("a warm glow slowly blooms and wraps the frame
  in gold"), timed to the swell.

## 8. Per-beat emotional-craft checklist (apply to EVERY beat)

Fill all five. If a row is blank or identical to the last beat, the beat is probably flat.

- **A. Emotion (one word).** If the muted keyframe doesn't say it, restage before generating.
- **B. Keyframe composition** — stack ≥3 agreeing channels: eyes (size + direction + catchlight);
  soft parts/posture (droop vs perk, slump/squash vs lift/stretch, pushed 120–150%); silhouette reads
  as a black shape, curved & asymmetric; scale/framing (small-in-space vs close-up — and *different*
  from last beat); camera height; color & light (temperature along the arc, key dark→lit, one warm
  accent if turning).
- **C. Motion prompt:** a speed word (eased); anticipation on actions; one secondary action; follow-
  through on soft parts; aliveness baseline even on holds; a verb of becoming on transformation beats.
- **D. Duration (varied):** differs from neighbors; holds/payoff are LONGEST (2.5–4s), actions shorter;
  peak is slowest (ritardando); one near-silent "take" before the big reaction.
- **E. Continuity/music:** eyeline + pose direction + color carry over; key moment lands on a music cue.

**Arc check across the whole piece — all five vectors travel beat 1 → beat 7:**
cool→warm ✓ · dark→light ✓ · small/far→close ✓ · slumped→lifted ✓ · fast→slow-at-peak ✓.

# PART 2 — Story structure & the emotional story spines

**Diagnosis first:** 4 near-static beats + a caption each = a *slideshow*. Emotion doesn't come from
*states* ("sad," then "happy") — it comes from **change caused by the character's own action**. Old
pieces were "Every day, every day, every day" with no **causality**. The fix is *causal chaining*
(each beat forces the next via "**because of that**") + a single mid-piece **turn**.

## The three structures, compressed to ~5 beats / ~20s

- **A. Pixar Story Spine** — *Once upon a time… Every day… **Until one day**… **Because of that**…
  Until finally… Ever since.* Best when Little One *does something* to change its world (causal drive).
  Compress to 5 ~4s beats: (1) establish + small want [hook], (2) until one day / the disruption,
  (3) because of that — Little One *acts*, it costs something, (4) until finally — the turn pays off,
  (5) ever since — new normal, resonant hold. **The magic word is "because of that"** — it converts a
  list into a chain.
- **B. Kishōtenketsu** (4-act, *conflict-free* — most "Mirror"-native): **Ki** (intro) → **Shō**
  (develop, grow attached) → **Ten** (an *unexpected element enters* — not opposed, just new) → **Ketsu**
  (reconcile; re-read the opening in a new light). Feeling comes from the **Ten** reframe, no villain.
- **C. Setup → Turn → Payoff** — tightest gut-punch. Setup (who+want ~6s) → Turn (pivot/reveal ~7s) →
  Payoff (earned beat ~7s). Rule #7 hardest here: **build the payoff first, reverse-engineer the setup.**

Pick: Spine for *journeys*, Kishōtenketsu for *quiet reframes*, Setup-Turn-Payoff for *one clean punch*.

## Pixar's 22 Rules — the 8 for a wordless 20s piece (Emma Coats, 2011)

- **#1** *"You admire a character for trying more than for their successes."* → show Little One
  *effortfully* wanting (reaching, straining). Effort = empathy; the empathy engine.
- **#2** audience-interesting > fun-to-make → kill the beautiful-but-inert keyframe.
- **#4** the story spine (above).
- **#5** *"Simplify. Focus… it sets you free."* → **one** feeling, **one** want, **one** turn. You fix
  flatness by *narrowing*, then giving that one thing full motion.
- **#7** *"Come up with your ending before your middle."* → decide the last frame's feeling first,
  storyboard backward.
- **#14** the burning belief = the brand: **you are enough once you truly see yourself.**
- **#15** animate the *honest* micro-reaction (the flinch before the smile).
- **#16** *"What are the stakes?"* → even gently, name the small stake (staying unseen / not-enough) so
  the payoff is *earned*.

## Want vs Need + empathy in 3 seconds

**Want** = external, visible, animatable ("reach that light"). **Need** = the emotional truth
underneath ("accept myself"). Studios make you feel by letting the character *chase the want* while the
film *delivers the need* — the turn is often when the want is revealed as the wrong thing. Mirror's
recurring shape: **Little One wants to be *more*; it needs to see it was already enough.**

**Save-the-cat in 0–3s** (do it *physically*, not as a pose — pick one): vulnerability (small in an
empty frame, hugs itself, tuft droops); longing (strains on tiptoe-stubs for something out of reach —
desire made visible); a tender act (cups something fragile); or a readable expressive face in frame 1
(>70% of viewers decide to stay within 3s). **Show the want or vulnerability as an ACTION, not a pose.**

## What makes wordless shorts land (Piper, Paperman, For the Birds, La Luna, Feast, John Lewis)

Single-emotion focus (one feeling, full-strength) · a universal relatable moment (Mirror's: *the gap
between how I see myself and what's true*) · **the reversal/turn is non-negotiable** (a wordless piece
with no turn is a mood board) · earned payoff via causality (cause→effect→consequence) · **music
carries the arc** (John Lewis: dialogue-free, music sets the tone → £1.2B incremental sales; the score
is your *voiceover* — land the swell exactly on the turn) · interiority through the body (a consistent
micro-gesture vocabulary) · the *other* is often the real subject (give Little One a second presence —
its reflection, a light — to be *seen by*).

## The turn — the single most important beat

Build one emotional state, then **cause** it to flip — the feeling is generated *in the flip*, not in
either state. Place the turn at **~55–65% of runtime** (~11–14s in a 20s piece) so the payoff can
breathe, and **land it on the music's peak.** Mirror-native pivots: alone→seen, not-enough→enough,
fear→safety, blind-spot→recognition.

## Short-form specifics

First 1–3s is everything; open on an expressive face + an immediate small action or an intriguing
*wrongness* (Little One waves, the reflection doesn't — the brain halts to resolve the mismatch: the
scroll-stopper). One clear feeling. Design ONE frame as the shareable "screenshot-able heart" (also the
thumbnail). End on resonance: hold the final emotional frame 1.5–2s longer than comfortable, let the
music resolve, brand/idea lands *after* the feeling, never during.

## Three buildable story concepts (wordless, ~20s, 5–7 beats)

### A — "The Wave" · fear → safety → self-acceptance · Story Spine
*Your reflection isn't your enemy.*
1. **(0–3s) longing+vulnerability** — Little One alone at bottom of a tall dark frame, reaches up on
   tiptoe-stubs toward its dim reflection above. *strain, tuft trembling.*
2. **(3–7s) fear** — the reflection ripples and looms, distorted, bigger. It flinches, covers eyes.
   *recoil, body deflates.*
3. **(7–11s) courage** — it peeks, then slowly reaches one stub to touch the surface. *hesitant, held breath.*
4. **(11–15s) THE TURN — recognition** — on contact the ripples still; the reflection settles and
   *mirrors its gesture gently.* Not a monster — me. *music peak; palms meet, glow blooms.*
5. **(15–19s) safety/joy** — cheeks brighten, tuft perks, the two press together, dark frame warms.
6. **(19–22s) held** — Little One turns from the mirror to look softly at the lens. Music resolves.

### B — "Not Enough" · not-enough → enough · Kishōtenketsu (conflict-free)
*You were already enough; you just couldn't see it.*
1. **(0–4s, Ki) wistful** — Little One watches a bright tall dazzling shape drift past above, looks
   down at its small grey self. *slight deflate.*
2. **(4–8s, Shō) yearning/effort** — it tries to *be* the bright thing: puffs up, stretches, fails,
   tries again. Endearing effort. *comic-tender stretching, small collapses.*
3. **(8–13s, Ten) a mirror appears** — it slumps before it, expecting "not enough." *trudge, sit, sigh.*
4. **(13–17s, Ten resolving) THE REVEAL** — in the reflection it finally sees what *we* saw: a soft
   warm glow it was casting all along, on its own back. It never noticed its light. *music peak; eyes
   widen, glow shimmers in.*
5. **(17–20s, Ketsu) enough** — it touches the glow, cheeks flush, sits up straight — not bigger, just
   *itself*. The bright shape drifts past again; now it just smiles, unbothered.
6. **(20–22s) held** — looks at us, calm. Re-read beat 1: it was always glowing.

### C — "Seen" · alone → seen · Setup–Turn–Payoff (tightest, most shareable — purest Mirror piece)
*Being truly seen by yourself.*
1. **(0–3s) loneliness** — Little One tiny in a vast empty pale frame, hugging itself, waves a small
   stub. Nothing answers. *wave, wilt.*
2. **(3–8s) searching** — it wanders, peeks behind/around, tuft alert then drooping each time. *hopeful
   peeks → small deflations (build the ache).*
3. **(8–11s) MISMATCH (scroll-stop)** — a mirror-surface fades in. It waves; the reflection is a beat
   *late*, then waves *back on its own.* It freezes. *wave, delayed answer, double-take.*
4. **(11–16s) THE TURN — being seen** — the reflection steps *toward the glass*, presses a stub to it,
   and *truly looks at* Little One — a companion: itself, meeting itself. *music peak; palm-to-palm,
   eyes lock, glow at contact.*
5. **(16–20s) belonging/relief** — cheeks bloom, both settle side by side against the glass, no longer
   alone; the empty frame fills with warmth.
6. **(20–22s) held** — Little One rests against its reflection, eyes closing, content.

**Build notes (all three):** storyboard the *final feeling* frame first (#7); give every beat a *cause*
from the last ("because it flinched, it peeked"); lock the **turn to the music's peak** before timing;
keep the gesture vocabulary consistent (tuft=hope, cheeks=joy, stubs-over-eyes=fear, body-round=defeat);
one feeling per film (A fear→safety, B not-enough→enough, C alone→seen — don't blend); brand lands only
in the final 2s.
